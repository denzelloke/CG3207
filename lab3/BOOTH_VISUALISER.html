Technical notes

Both inputs are signed 4-bit numbers: −8 to +7. A has one extra bit, so even −8 works. M keeps its value by copying its sign: .

The two bits are Q’s last bit, then q₀. In your packed register these are booth[1:0]. The old Q[0] slides into q₀. This is sometimes labelled q₁ → q₀ in a packed diagram.

We do exactly four rounds. At the end, drop A’s extra sign bit and q₀. Join A’s last four bits to Q. That makes the signed 8-bit answer.

Taking away M is the same as adding −M. To make −M in binary, flip M’s five bits and add 1. The animation then adds from right to left, passing each carry to the next box. Any carry beyond five bits is left out.
